# 🏗️ Time and Work

## Introduction

Time and Work is one of the most important and frequently asked topics in
Quantitative Aptitude. It deals with the relationship between the amount of work
done, the number of workers, and the time taken to complete a task.

Questions from this topic are common in SSC, Banking, Railways, CAT, UPSC,
placement tests, and other competitive exams.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After completing this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand the concept of work efficiency
- Calculate time taken to complete work
- Solve problems involving multiple workers
- Handle work and wages questions
- Solve pipe and cistern problems
- Apply shortcuts to solve aptitude questions quickly

---

# Basic Concept

Work is directly proportional to:

```text id="tw-1"
Efficiency × Time
```

Relationship:

```text id="tw-2"
Work = Efficiency × Time
```

---

## Important Rule

If a person can complete a work in:

```text id="tw-3"
n days
```

Then one day's work is:

```text id="tw-4"
1/n
```

---

## Example

A can complete a work in:

```text id="tw-5"
10 days
```

One day's work:

```text id="tw-6"
1/10
```

This means A completes:

```text id="tw-7"
10%
```

of the work every day.

---

# Work Formula

The fundamental relationship is:

Work = Efficiency \times Time

From this:

### Time

```text id="tw-8"
Time = Work / Efficiency
```

### Efficiency

```text id="tw-9"
Efficiency = Work / Time
```

---

# Finding Total Work Using LCM

A very useful method is to assume total work as the LCM of individual completion
times.

---

## Example

A completes work in:

```text id="tw-10"
10 days
```

B completes work in:

```text id="tw-11"
15 days
```

Take total work as:

```text id="tw-12"
LCM(10,15)

= 30 units
```

Efficiency:

```text id="tw-13"
A = 30/10 = 3 units/day

B = 30/15 = 2 units/day
```

Combined efficiency:

```text id="tw-14"
3 + 2

= 5 units/day
```

Time required:

```text id="tw-15"
30/5

= 6 days
```

**Answer:** 6 days

---

# Work Done Together

If A completes work in:

```text id="tw-16"
a days
```

and B completes work in:

```text id="tw-17"
b days
```

Then together:

```text id="tw-18"
1/a + 1/b
```

work is completed per day.

---

## Formula

```text id="tw-19"
Time Taken

= (ab)/(a+b)
```

Applicable when only two workers are involved.

---

## Example

A completes work in:

```text id="tw-20"
12 days
```

B completes work in:

```text id="tw-21"
18 days
```

Together:

```text id="tw-22"
(12 × 18)/(12 + 18)

= 216/30

= 7.2 days
```

**Answer:** 7.2 days

---

# Efficiency Concept

Efficiency refers to the amount of work completed per unit time.

More efficiency means less time.

Less efficiency means more time.

---

## Example

If A is twice as efficient as B:

```text id="tw-23"
Efficiency Ratio

A : B = 2 : 1
```

Then time ratio becomes:

```text id="tw-24"
B : A = 1 : 2
```

Time is inversely proportional to efficiency.

---

# Work and Wages

Wages are distributed according to work done.

---

## Example

A and B work in ratio:

```text id="tw-25"
3 : 2
```

Total wage:

```text id="tw-26"
₹5000
```

A's share:

```text id="tw-27"
(3/5) × 5000

= ₹3000
```

B's share:

```text id="tw-28"
(2/5) × 5000

= ₹2000
```

---

# Pipes and Cisterns

Pipe questions follow the same Time and Work concepts.

---

## Filling Pipe

If a pipe fills a tank in:

```text id="tw-29"
10 hours
```

Then one hour's work:

```text id="tw-30"
1/10
```

---

## Emptying Pipe

If a pipe empties a tank in:

```text id="tw-31"
20 hours
```

Then one hour's work:

```text id="tw-32"
-1/20
```

Negative sign indicates removal of work.

---

## Example

Pipe A fills tank in:

```text id="tw-33"
12 hours
```

Pipe B empties tank in:

```text id="tw-34"
18 hours
```

Net work:

```text id="tw-35"
1/12 - 1/18
```

LCM:

```text id="tw-36"
(3 - 2)/36

= 1/36
```

Time required:

```text id="tw-37"
36 hours
```

**Answer:** 36 hours

---

# Men, Women, and Children Problems

## Example

```text id="tw-38"
2 Men = 3 Women
```

Then efficiency ratio:

```text id="tw-39"
Men : Women

= 3 : 2
```

This ratio can be used to solve work-related questions.

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

A completes work in:

```text id="tw-40"
20 days
```

Find one day's work.

Solution:

```text id="tw-41"
1/20
```

**Answer:** 1/20

---

## Example 2

A can do a job in 15 days and B in 10 days.

Find time taken together.

Solution:

```text id="tw-42"
(15 × 10)/(15 + 10)

= 150/25

= 6 days
```

**Answer:** 6 days

---

## Example 3

A is three times as efficient as B.

A takes:

```text id="tw-43"
8 days
```

Find B's time.

Solution:

Efficiency ratio:

```text id="tw-44"
A : B

= 3 : 1
```

Time ratio:

```text id="tw-45"
1 : 3
```

Therefore:

```text id="tw-46"
B = 24 days
```

**Answer:** 24 days

---

## Example 4

A pipe fills a tank in:

```text id="tw-47"
8 hours
```

Find one hour's work.

Solution:

```text id="tw-48"
1/8
```

**Answer:** 1/8

---

## Example 5

A and B earn ₹9000 in ratio:

```text id="tw-49"
5 : 4
```

Find A's share.

Solution:

```text id="tw-50"
(5/9) × 9000

= ₹5000
```

**Answer:** ₹5000

# Practice Questions

## Easy

1. A completes work in 12 days. Find one day's work.
2. B completes work in 18 days. Find one day's work.
3. A and B complete work in 15 and 10 days. Find time together.
4. A pipe fills a tank in 20 hours. Find one hour's work.
5. Divide ₹6000 in ratio 2 : 3.

---

## Medium

1. A completes work in 24 days and B in 12 days. Find time together.
2. A is twice as efficient as B. If A takes 10 days, find B's time.
3. Pipe A fills a tank in 15 hours and Pipe B fills it in 10 hours. Find total
   time.
4. Divide ₹12000 in ratio 7 : 5.
5. A and B can complete a task in 8 and 12 days respectively. Find combined
   efficiency.

---

## Hard

1. A, B, and C can complete a task in 12, 18, and 24 days. Find time together.
2. A pipe fills a tank in 10 hours and another empties it in 15 hours. Find net
   filling time.
3. A is 50% more efficient than B. If B takes 18 days, find A's time.
4. A contractor pays ₹45,000 among workers in ratio 4 : 3 : 2. Find each share.
5. A completes work in 15 days. B is twice as efficient as A. Find time taken by
   B.

# Quick Tricks

✅ If a person completes work in **n days**, one day's work is:

```text id="tw-51"
1/n
```

---

✅ Efficiency and Time are inversely proportional.

```text id="tw-52"
More Efficiency → Less Time

Less Efficiency → More Time
```

---

✅ For two workers:

```text id="tw-53"
Together Time

= (ab)/(a+b)
```

---

✅ Pipes and Cisterns use the same Time & Work concepts.

---

✅ Use LCM method for faster calculations.

---

# Common Exam Mistakes

❌ Adding completion times directly.

❌ Confusing efficiency ratio with time ratio.

❌ Forgetting that emptying pipes contribute negatively.

❌ Not converting work into fractions properly.

---

# Key Takeaways

- One day's work = 1 / Total Days.
- Work = Efficiency × Time.
- Efficiency and Time are inversely proportional.
- Combined work is calculated by adding individual work rates.
- Pipes and Cisterns are applications of Time & Work.
- LCM method simplifies complex questions.
- Time and Work is one of the most scoring aptitude topics in competitive exams.
