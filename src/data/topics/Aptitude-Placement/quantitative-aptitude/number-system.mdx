# 🔢 Number System

## Introduction

The Number System is the foundation of Quantitative Aptitude. Almost every aptitude topic directly or indirectly depends on number properties and arithmetic operations.

Understanding the Number System helps in solving problems related to divisibility, factors, multiples, remainders, HCF, LCM, and many advanced aptitude concepts.

# What is a Number?

A number is a mathematical value used for counting, measuring, and performing calculations.

Examples:

```text
1, 5, 10, 25, 100
```

---

# Classification of Numbers

## Natural Numbers (N)

Numbers used for counting.

```text
1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...
```

### Properties

* Smallest natural number = 1
* No largest natural number
* Infinite in count

---

## Whole Numbers (W)

Natural numbers including zero.

```text
0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...
```

### Properties

* Smallest whole number = 0
* Infinite in count

---

## Integers (Z)

Positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero.

```text
..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
```

### Types

#### Positive Integers

```text
1, 2, 3, 4, ...
```

#### Negative Integers

```text
-1, -2, -3, -4, ...
```

---

## Rational Numbers

Numbers that can be expressed as:

```text
p/q
```

Where:

* p = Integer
* q ≠ 0

Examples:

```text
1/2
3/4
5/8
7
```

(7 can be written as 7/1)

---

## Irrational Numbers

Numbers that cannot be expressed as p/q.

Examples:

```text
√2
√3
π
```

Their decimal expansion never ends and never repeats.

---

## Real Numbers

Combination of:

* Rational Numbers
* Irrational Numbers

Examples:

```text
-5
0
3.25
√2
π
```

---

# Even and Odd Numbers

## Even Numbers

Numbers divisible by 2.

Examples:

```text
2, 4, 6, 8, 10
```

### Formula

```text
2n
```

where n is an integer.

---

## Odd Numbers

Numbers not divisible by 2.

Examples:

```text
1, 3, 5, 7, 9
```

### Formula

```text
2n + 1
```

---

# Prime Numbers

A prime number has exactly two factors:

1. 1
2. The number itself

Examples:

```text
2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17
```

### Important Points

* 2 is the only even prime number.
* 1 is not a prime number.
* Every prime number greater than 2 is odd.

---

# Composite Numbers

Numbers having more than two factors.

Examples:

```text
4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12
```

### Example

Factors of 12:

```text
1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
```

Since it has more than two factors, it is composite.

---

### Example

Factors of 12:

```text
1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
```

---

## Multiples

Numbers obtained by multiplying a number.

### Example

Multiples of 5:

```text
5, 10, 15, 20, 25, ...
```

# Divisibility Rules

## Divisible by 2

Last digit must be even.

Examples:

```text
246 ✓
138 ✓
```

---

## Divisible by 3

Sum of digits should be divisible by 3.

Example:

```text
123

1 + 2 + 3 = 6

6 is divisible by 3
```

Therefore:

```text
123 ✓
```

---

## Divisible by 4

Last two digits should be divisible by 4.

Example:

```text
1216

16 ÷ 4 = 4
```

Therefore:

```text
1216 ✓
```

---

## Divisible by 5

Last digit should be:

```text
0 or 5
```

Examples:

```text
125 ✓
230 ✓
```

---

## Divisible by 6

Number must be divisible by:

```text
2 and 3
```

Both conditions should be satisfied.

---

## Divisible by 8

Last three digits should be divisible by 8.

Example:

```text
2048

048 = 48

48 ÷ 8 = 6
```

Therefore divisible by 8.

---

## Divisible by 9

Sum of digits should be divisible by 9.

Example:

```text
729

7 + 2 + 9 = 18

18 ÷ 9 = 2
```

Therefore divisible by 9.

---

## Divisible by 10

Last digit should be:

```text
0
```

Example:

```text
560 ✓
```

# HCF (Highest Common Factor)

The greatest number that divides all given numbers.

### Example

Find HCF of:

```text
12 and 18
```

Factors:

```text
12 = 1,2,3,4,6,12

18 = 1,2,3,6,9,18
```

Common factors:

```text
1,2,3,6
```

Highest common factor:

```text
HCF = 6
```

---

# LCM (Least Common Multiple)

The smallest number divisible by all given numbers.

### Example

Find LCM of:

```text
12 and 18
```

Multiples:

```text
12 → 12,24,36,48,...

18 → 18,36,54,...
```

Common smallest multiple:

```text
36
```

Therefore:

```text
LCM = 36
```

---

# Relationship Between HCF and LCM

For two numbers:

```text
HCF × LCM = Product of Numbers
```

### Example

Numbers:

```text
12 and 18
```

```text
6 × 36 = 216

12 × 18 = 216
```

Verified.

---

# Remainder Concept

When a number does not divide completely, the leftover part is called the remainder.

### Example

```text
17 ÷ 5
```

```text
5 × 3 = 15

Remainder = 2
```
