# ⚖️ Ratios and Proportions

## Introduction

Ratios and Proportions are fundamental concepts in Quantitative Aptitude. They
are used to compare quantities, distribute values, solve partnership problems,
mixture problems, ages, percentages, and many real-life situations.

A strong understanding of ratios and proportions makes many aptitude questions
easier and faster to solve.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After completing this chapter, you will be able to:

- Understand ratios and proportions
- Simplify ratios
- Compare quantities efficiently
- Solve direct and inverse proportion problems
- Divide quantities in a given ratio
- Solve aptitude questions involving ages, mixtures, and partnerships

---

# What is a Ratio?

A ratio is a comparison of two quantities of the same kind.

It shows how many times one quantity contains another.

### Representation

```text id="ratio-1"
a : b
```

or

```text id="ratio-2"
a/b
```

Where:

- a = First term (Antecedent)
- b = Second term (Consequent)

---

## Example

If there are:

```text id="ratio-3"
10 boys
20 girls
```

Ratio of boys to girls:

```text id="ratio-4"
10 : 20
```

Simplifying:

```text id="ratio-5"
1 : 2
```

**Answer:** 1 : 2

---

# Simplifying Ratios

To simplify a ratio, divide both terms by their HCF.

---

## Example

Simplify:

```text id="ratio-6"
24 : 36
```

HCF:

```text id="ratio-7"
12
```

Divide both terms:

```text id="ratio-8"
24 ÷ 12 : 36 ÷ 12

= 2 : 3
```

**Answer:** 2 : 3

---

# Equivalent Ratios

Ratios that represent the same relationship are called equivalent ratios.

### Example

```text id="ratio-9"
1 : 2

2 : 4

3 : 6

5 : 10
```

All are equivalent.

---

# Comparing Ratios

Convert ratios into fractions and compare.

### Example

Compare:

```text id="ratio-10"
3 : 4

5 : 6
```

Fractions:

```text id="ratio-11"
3/4 = 0.75

5/6 ≈ 0.833
```

Therefore:

```text id="ratio-12"
5 : 6 > 3 : 4
```

---

# What is Proportion?

When two ratios are equal, they form a proportion.

Representation:

```text id="ratio-13"
a : b = c : d
```

or

```text id="ratio-14"
a/b = c/d
```

---

## Example

```text id="ratio-15"
2 : 4 = 3 : 6
```

Because:

```text id="ratio-16"
2/4 = 3/6 = 1/2
```

This is a proportion.

---

# Property of Proportion

For:

```text id="ratio-17"
a : b = c : d
```

Then:

```text id="ratio-18"
a × d = b × c
```

This is called cross multiplication.

---

## Example

```text id="ratio-19"
3 : 5 = x : 20
```

Cross multiply:

```text id="ratio-20"
3 × 20 = 5 × x

60 = 5x

x = 12
```

**Answer:** 12

---

# Continued Proportion

If:

```text id="ratio-21"
a : b = b : c
```

Then:

```text id="ratio-22"
b² = ac
```

---

## Example

```text id="ratio-23"
4 : 8 = 8 : 16
```

Check:

```text id="ratio-24"
8² = 4 × 16

64 = 64
```

Correct.

---

# Direct Proportion

Two quantities are in direct proportion if one increases and the other also
increases.

---

## Example

More workers → More work completed.

More distance → More fuel consumed.

---

### Formula

```text id="ratio-25"
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂
```

---

### Example

If 5 books cost ₹200,

What is the cost of 8 books?

Solution:

```text id="ratio-26"
5/8 = 200/x
```

Cross multiply:

```text id="ratio-27"
5x = 1600

x = ₹320
```

**Answer:** ₹320

---

# Inverse Proportion

Two quantities are inversely proportional if one increases and the other
decreases.

---

## Example

More workers → Less time required.

Higher speed → Less travel time.

---

### Formula

```text id="ratio-28"
a₁ × b₁ = a₂ × b₂
```

---

### Example

10 workers complete work in 12 days.

How many days will 15 workers take?

Solution:

```text id="ratio-29"
10 × 12 = 15 × x
```

```text id="ratio-30"
120 = 15x

x = 8
```

**Answer:** 8 days

---

# Dividing a Quantity in a Ratio

If a quantity is to be divided in the ratio:

```text id="ratio-31"
a : b
```

Then total parts:

```text id="ratio-32"
a + b
```

---

## Example

Divide ₹900 in ratio:

```text id="ratio-33"
2 : 3
```

Total parts:

```text id="ratio-34"
2 + 3 = 5
```

First share:

```text id="ratio-35"
(2/5) × 900

= ₹360
```

Second share:

```text id="ratio-36"
(3/5) × 900

= ₹540
```

---

# Ratio of Ages

Age problems frequently use ratios.

---

## Example

Present ages of A and B are:

```text id="ratio-37"
3 : 5
```

Total age:

```text id="ratio-38"
64 years
```

Find ages.

Solution:

Total parts:

```text id="ratio-39"
3 + 5 = 8
```

One part:

```text id="ratio-40"
64 ÷ 8

= 8
```

A's age:

```text id="ratio-41"
3 × 8

= 24
```

B's age:

```text id="ratio-42"
5 × 8

= 40
```

**Answer:** 24 years and 40 years

---

# Partnership Problems

Profit is distributed according to investment ratio.

---

## Example

A invests:

```text id="ratio-43"
₹40,000
```

B invests:

```text id="ratio-44"
₹60,000
```

Profit:

```text id="ratio-45"
₹20,000
```

Investment ratio:

```text id="ratio-46"
40000 : 60000

= 2 : 3
```

Total parts:

```text id="ratio-47"
5
```

A's share:

```text id="ratio-48"
(2/5) × 20000

= ₹8000
```

B's share:

```text id="ratio-49"
(3/5) × 20000

= ₹12000
```

# Solved Examples

## Example 1

Simplify:

```text id="ratio-50"
18 : 30
```

Solution:

```text id="ratio-51"
HCF = 6

18 ÷ 6 : 30 ÷ 6

= 3 : 5
```

**Answer:** 3 : 5

---

## Example 2

Find x:

```text id="ratio-52"
4 : 7 = x : 21
```

Solution:

```text id="ratio-53"
4 × 21 = 7x

84 = 7x

x = 12
```

**Answer:** 12

---

## Example 3

Divide ₹600 in ratio:

```text id="ratio-54"
1 : 2
```

Solution:

```text id="ratio-55"
Total parts = 3
```

```text id="ratio-56"
First share = ₹200

Second share = ₹400
```

---

## Example 4

12 workers complete work in 15 days.

How many days for 20 workers?

Solution:

```text id="ratio-57"
12 × 15 = 20 × x
```

```text id="ratio-58"
180 = 20x

x = 9
```

**Answer:** 9 days

# Quick Tricks

✅ Ratio remains unchanged if both terms are multiplied or divided by the same
number.

✅ For direct proportion:

```text id="ratio-59"
Increase ↔ Increase

Decrease ↔ Decrease
```

✅ For inverse proportion:

```text id="ratio-60"
Increase ↔ Decrease
```

✅ Always simplify ratios before calculations.

✅ Use cross multiplication for proportion questions.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Ratio compares two quantities.
- Proportion means equality of two ratios.
- Cross multiplication is the most important proportion technique.
- Direct proportion moves together.
- Inverse proportion moves oppositely.
- Ratio concepts are heavily used in ages, mixtures, partnerships, and
  percentages.
- Mastering ratios makes many aptitude problems easier and faster.
