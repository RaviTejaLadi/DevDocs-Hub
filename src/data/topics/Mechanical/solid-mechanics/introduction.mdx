# 📖 Introduction to Solid Mechanics

Solid Mechanics is the branch of engineering that studies how solid materials
behave when subjected to forces, loads, temperature changes, and other external
effects.

It helps engineers design structures and machine components that are safe,
strong, and durable.

Examples include:

- 🏗️ Buildings and bridges
- 🚗 Automobile components
- ✈️ Aircraft structures
- ⚙️ Machine shafts and gears
- 🚢 Ships and offshore structures

---

# 🎯 Why Study Solid Mechanics?

Engineers must ensure that components:

- Do not break under load
- Do not deform excessively
- Remain stable during operation
- Have sufficient safety margins

Solid mechanics provides the tools to analyze and design such components.

---

# 🔍 Basic Concept

Whenever a force acts on a body, the body tends to deform.

Examples:

- Stretching a rubber band
- Compressing a spring
- Bending a ruler
- Twisting a screwdriver

The relationship between applied force and resulting deformation is the
foundation of solid mechanics.

---

# ⚡ Fundamental Elements

## Force

A push or pull acting on a body.

Unit:

```
Newton (N)
```

Examples:

- Weight of an object
- Tension in a cable
- Pressure on a wall

---

## Load

External force applied to a structure.

Types:

- Point Load
- Distributed Load
- Impact Load
- Dynamic Load

---

## Deformation

Change in shape or size caused by loading.

Examples:

- Elongation
- Compression
- Bending
- Twisting

---

# 🏗️ Classification of Bodies

## Rigid Body

Assumes no deformation under load.

Used in:

- Engineering Mechanics
- Statics
- Dynamics

Example:

A steel block considered perfectly rigid.

---

## Deformable Body

Undergoes deformation when forces act.

Real engineering materials belong to this category.

Examples:

- Steel rods
- Concrete beams
- Aluminum plates

---

# 🔬 Material Behavior

Materials respond differently to loads.

## Elastic Material

Returns to original shape after load removal.

Examples:

- Spring steel
- Rubber (within limits)

---

## Plastic Material

Does not fully return to original shape.

Example:

- Mild steel beyond yield point

---

## Brittle Material

Fails suddenly with little deformation.

Examples:

- Glass
- Cast iron

---

## Ductile Material

Shows large deformation before failure.

Examples:

- Steel
- Copper
- Aluminum

# 📝 Key Objectives of Solid Mechanics

Engineers use solid mechanics to determine:

1. Internal forces developed in structures
2. Stresses produced due to loading
3. Deformations caused by forces
4. Strength of materials
5. Safe dimensions of components

---

# 🎓 Summary

Solid Mechanics studies the behavior of solid materials under external forces.

The subject helps engineers analyze:

- Stress
- Strain
- Deformation
- Strength
- Stability

It forms the foundation for structural engineering, machine design, aerospace
engineering, civil engineering, and manufacturing industries.
