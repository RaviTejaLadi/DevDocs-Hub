# Heat & Temperature

## Introduction to Thermal Physics

Thermal physics deals with heat, temperature, and their effects on matter. It
bridges microscopic atomic behavior with macroscopic observations.

### What is Temperature?

Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of particles in a
substance. It determines the direction of heat flow between objects in thermal
contact.

### Thermal Equilibrium and Zeroth Law

- **Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics**: If two systems are each in thermal
  equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each
  other.
- This law establishes temperature as a fundamental measurable quantity.
- Thermal equilibrium occurs when no net heat flows between objects.

#### Celsius Scale (°C)

- Based on freezing (0°C) and boiling (100°C) points of water at 1 atm
- Widely used in everyday applications

#### Fahrenheit Scale (°F)

- 32°F = freezing point, 212°F = boiling point of water
- Common in United States

#### Kelvin Scale (K)

- Absolute temperature scale
- 0 K = absolute zero (-273.15°C)
- No negative temperatures
- K = °C + 273.15

#### Conversion Formulas

```
°C = (°F - 32) × 5/9
°F = °C × 9/5 + 32
K = °C + 273.15
```

#### Types of Thermometers

- **Liquid-in-glass**: Mercury or alcohol expansion
- **Resistance thermometer**: Electrical resistance changes with temperature
- **Thermocouple**: Voltage difference between two dissimilar metals
- **Infrared thermometer**: Measures thermal radiation
- **Bimetallic strip**: Differential expansion of two metals

### Linear Expansion

When a solid is heated, its length increases proportionally to the original
length and temperature change.

```
ΔL = αL₀ΔT
```

Where:

- α = coefficient of linear expansion (K⁻¹)
- L₀ = original length
- ΔT = change in temperature

### Area Expansion

```
ΔA = 2αA₀ΔT
```

### Volume Expansion

```
ΔV = βV₀ΔT
```

For most solids: β ≈ 3α

### Coefficients of Thermal Expansion

| Material      | α (×10⁻⁶ K⁻¹)      |
| ------------- | ------------------ |
| Aluminum      | 23.1               |
| Copper        | 16.5               |
| Steel         | 11.5               |
| Glass (Pyrex) | 3.2                |
| Invar (alloy) | 1.0                |
| Water         | 210 (liquid, 20°C) |

### Applications and Consequences

- **Expansion joints** in bridges and railways
- **Bimetallic strips** in thermostats
- **Tightening of bolts** when heated
- **Thermal stress** in structures
- **Anomalous expansion of water**: Water expands when cooled below 4°C, causing
  ice to float

### What is Heat?

Heat is the transfer of thermal energy between systems due to temperature
difference. It flows from hot to cold objects.

### Units of Heat

- **Joule (J)**: SI unit
- **Calorie (cal)**: 1 cal = 4.186 J
- **British Thermal Unit (BTU)**: 1 BTU = 1055 J

### Specific Heat Capacity (c)

The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1
K.

```
Q = mcΔT
```

### Specific Heats of Common Substances

| Substance | Specific Heat (J/kg·K) |
| --------- | ---------------------- |
| Water     | 4186                   |
| Ice       | 2100                   |
| Aluminum  | 900                    |
| Copper    | 385                    |
| Iron      | 450                    |
| Air       | 1005                   |

### Molar Specific Heat

For gases:

- At constant volume: C_v
- At constant pressure: C_p
- C_p - C_v = R (universal gas constant)

### Calorimetry Principle

Heat lost = Heat gained (in a closed system)

```
m₁c₁ΔT₁ = m₂c₂ΔT₂
```

### Water Equivalent

Mass of water that would require the same heat as the calorimeter for same
temperature change.

### Phase Transitions

| Phase Change   | Process        | Latent Heat                       |
| -------------- | -------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Solid → Liquid | Melting/Fusion | Latent heat of fusion (L_f)       |
| Liquid → Solid | Freezing       | L_f                               |
| Liquid → Gas   | Vaporization   | Latent heat of vaporization (L_v) |
| Gas → Liquid   | Condensation   | L_v                               |
| Solid → Gas    | Sublimation    | Latent heat of sublimation        |
| Gas → Solid    | Deposition     | Latent heat of sublimation        |

### Latent Heat Equations

```
Q = mL
```

- L_f = 334 kJ/kg (for water)
- L_v = 2260 kJ/kg (for water at 100°C)

### Heating/Cooling Curve

- **Plateaus** occur during phase changes
- Temperature remains constant during phase transition
- Energy goes into changing phase, not increasing temperature

### Triple Point and Critical Point

- **Triple point**: Temperature and pressure where solid, liquid, and gas
  coexist
- **Critical point**: Temperature above which gas cannot be liquefied by
  pressure alone

### Boiling and Evaporation

- **Boiling**: Occurs throughout liquid when vapor pressure = atmospheric
  pressure
- **Evaporation**: Surface phenomenon, occurs at any temperature
- Factors affecting evaporation rate: temperature, surface area, humidity, air
  movement



#### Fourier's Law of Conduction

```
P = (kAΔT)/L
```

- P = heat current (W)
- k = thermal conductivity (W/m·K)
- A = cross-sectional area
- L = length
- ΔT = temperature difference

#### Thermal Conductivity Values

| Material    | k (W/m·K) |
| ----------- | --------- |
| Silver      | 429       |
| Copper      | 401       |
| Aluminum    | 237       |
| Glass       | 0.8       |
| Water       | 0.6       |
| Wood        | 0.15      |
| Air         | 0.025     |
| Polystyrene | 0.03      |

#### R-Value and Thermal Resistance

```
R = L/k (for a layer)
Total R = sum of individual R-values
```

#### Natural Convection

- Caused by buoyancy forces from density differences
- Warm fluid rises, cool fluid sinks
- Examples: Heating water in a pot, atmospheric circulation

#### Forced Convection

- Fluid movement caused by external means (fan, pump)
- More efficient than natural convection

#### Heat Transfer Coefficient

```
P = hAΔT
```

Where h = convection heat transfer coefficient

#### Stefan-Boltzmann Law

```
P = εσAT⁴
```

- ε = emissivity (0 to 1)
- σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/m²·K⁴ (Stefan-Boltzmann constant)
- A = surface area
- T = absolute temperature

#### Wien's Displacement Law

```
λ_max = b/T
```

Where b = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K

#### Kirchhoff's Law

- Good absorbers are good emitters
- Blackbody: ε = 1 (perfect absorber and emitter)

#### Applications

- Solar radiation
- Greenhouse effect
- Thermal imaging
- Cooling of buildings

### First Law of Thermodynamics

```
ΔU = Q - W
```

- ΔU = change in internal energy
- Q = heat added to system
- W = work done by system

#### Isothermal Process (T = constant)

```
ΔU = 0
Q = W
W = nRT ln(V_f/V_i)
```

#### Isobaric Process (P = constant)

```
W = PΔV
ΔU = Q - PΔV
```

#### Isochoric Process (V = constant)

```
W = 0
ΔU = Q
Q = nC_vΔT
```

#### Adiabatic Process (Q = 0)

```
ΔU = -W
PV^γ = constant
T V^(γ-1) = constant
```

Where γ = C_p/C_v

#### Kelvin-Planck Statement

No cyclic process can convert all heat absorbed from a reservoir into work.

#### Clausius Statement

Heat cannot flow spontaneously from a cold body to a hot body.

#### Entropy

```
ΔS = Q_rev/T
```

- Entropy is a measure of disorder
- For isolated systems: ΔS ≥ 0
- Heat engines must increase total entropy

#### Carnot Engine (Ideal)

```
efficiency = 1 - T_C/T_H
```

Where T_H = hot reservoir temperature, T_C = cold reservoir temperature

#### Refrigerators and Heat Pumps

```
Coefficient of Performance (COP) = Q_C/W = Q_C/(Q_H - Q_C)
```

### Third Law of Thermodynamics

- As temperature approaches absolute zero, entropy approaches a minimum value
- Absolute zero cannot be reached in finite steps



#### Boyle's Law (T constant)

```
PV = constant
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂
```

#### Charles's Law (P constant)

```
V/T = constant
V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂
```

#### Gay-Lussac's Law (V constant)

```
P/T = constant
P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂
```

#### Ideal Gas Equation

```
PV = nRT
```

Where R = 8.314 J/mol·K

#### Root Mean Square Speed

```
v_rms = √(3RT/M)
```

#### Average Speed

```
v_avg = √(8RT/πM)
```

#### Most Probable Speed

```
v_mp = √(2RT/M)
```

### Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution

- Distribution of molecular speeds in a gas
- Depends on temperature and molecular mass

### Degrees of Freedom

- Monatomic: 3 translational
- Diatomic: 3 translational + 2 rotational
- Polyatomic: 3 translational + 3 rotational

### Molar Specific Heats

For ideal gases:

- C_v = (f/2)R
- C_p = C_v + R
