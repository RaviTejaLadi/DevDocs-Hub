# Work, Energy & Power

## Work

### Definition

Work is done when a force causes displacement of an object.

### Mathematical Expression

```
W = F⋅d⋅cos θ
```

Where:

- W = work done
- F = magnitude of force
- d = magnitude of displacement
- θ = angle between force and displacement

### Special Cases

- **Work done by constant force**: W = F⋅s
- **Work done by variable force**: W = ∫F(x)⋅dx
- **No work**: θ = 90° (perpendicular force)
- **Maximum work**: θ = 0° (parallel force)

### Units

- SI unit: joule (J) = N⋅m
- Other units: erg, foot-pound, calorie, electron-volt

## Energy

### Kinetic Energy

Energy of motion:

```
KE = ½mv²
```

### Potential Energy

Energy of position or configuration:

#### Gravitational Potential Energy

```
PE = mgh
```

(Where h is height above reference point)

#### Elastic Potential Energy

```
PE = ½kx²
```

(For spring, k = spring constant, x = displacement)

#### Work-Energy Theorem

```
W_net = ΔKE = ½mv_f² - ½mv_i²
```

### Conservation of Mechanical Energy

For conservative forces only:

```
KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f
```

### Types of Potential Energy

1. **Gravitational**: U = mgh
2. **Elastic**: U = ½kx²
3. **Electrostatic**: U = kq₁q₂/r
4. **Chemical**: Stored in bonds
5. **Nuclear**: Stored in nuclei

## Conservative vs Non-conservative Forces

### Conservative Forces

- Work independent of path
- Work depends only on initial and final positions
- Can define potential energy
- Examples: Gravity, spring force, electrostatic force

### Non-conservative Forces

- Work depends on path
- No potential energy defined
- Examples: Friction, air resistance, applied forces

### Work-Energy with Non-conservative Forces

```
W_nc = ΔKE + ΔPE
```

## Power

### Definition

Power is the rate of doing work or transferring energy.

### Equations

- Average power: P_avg = W/t
- Instantaneous power: P = dW/dt = F⋅v
- Electrical power: P = VI
- Mechanical power: P = τ⋅ω

### Units

- SI unit: watt (W) = J/s
- Horsepower: 1 hp = 746 W
- Kilowatt-hour: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J

## Types of Energy

### Thermal Energy

- Internal energy of substances
- Heat transfer mechanisms
- Specific heat capacity

### Nuclear Energy

- Fission: Heavy nuclei split
- Fusion: Light nuclei combine
- Mass-energy equivalence: E = mc²

### Light Energy

- Photons
- Electromagnetic radiation
- Energy = hf

### Sound Energy

- Mechanical waves
- Energy propagation through medium
- Intensity measured in decibels

## Energy Conservation in Systems

### Isolated Systems

- Total energy is constant
- Energy can transform between forms
- No energy created or destroyed

### Open Systems

- Energy can enter or leave
- Work and heat transfer

### Coefficient of Restitution

```
e = (v₂f - v₁f)/(v₁i - v₂i)
```

- e = 1: Perfectly elastic
- e = 0: Perfectly inelastic
- 0 < e < 1: Partially elastic

## Applications

### Simple Machines

- Lever: Mechanical advantage = d_in/d_out
- Pulley: MA = number of rope segments
- Inclined plane: MA = 1/sin θ
- Wheel and axle: MA = R/r
- Wedge: MA = length/thickness
- Screw: MA = 2πr/pitch

### Efficiency

```
Efficiency = (Useful output energy)/(Total input energy) × 100%
```

### Renewable vs Non-renewable Energy

- Solar, wind, hydro, geothermal
- Fossil fuels, nuclear
