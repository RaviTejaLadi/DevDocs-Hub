# Newton's Laws of Motion

## Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia)

### Statement

"An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with
constant velocity, unless acted upon by a net external force."

### Key Concepts

- **Inertia**: Property of matter resisting change in motion
- **Mass**: Measure of inertia (scalar quantity)
- **Equilibrium**: When net force = 0
- **Galileo's Principle**: Motion is relative

### Examples

- Objects on a frictionless surface continue moving
- Passengers lurch forward when brakes are applied
- Satellites in orbit continue in motion

## Newton's Second Law

### Statement

"The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting
on it and inversely proportional to its mass."

### Mathematical Form

```
F_net = ma
```

### Components

- F_x = ma_x
- F_y = ma_y
- F_z = ma_z

### Important Applications

1. **Force calculations**: F = m⋅a
2. **Weight calculation**: W = mg
3. **Mass determination**: m = F/a
4. **Acceleration determination**: a = F/m

## Newton's Third Law

### Statement

"For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."

### Key Points

- Forces always occur in pairs
- Action and reaction forces act on different objects
- Magnitudes are equal, directions are opposite
- These forces are of the same type (both contact, both gravitational, etc.)

### Examples

- Walking: Foot pushes ground backward, ground pushes foot forward
- Rocket propulsion: Exhaust gases pushed backward, rocket moves forward
- Book on table: Book pushes table, table pushes book

## Applications of Newton's Laws

### Motion on Inclined Planes

For an object on an incline:

- Parallel component: mg sin θ
- Perpendicular component: mg cos θ
- Normal force: N = mg cos θ
- Net force: F_net = mg sin θ - friction

### Atwood Machine

- Two masses connected by string over pulley
- Acceleration: a = (m₁ - m₂)g/(m₁ + m₂)
- Tension: T = 2m₁m₂g/(m₁ + m₂)

### Elevator Problems

- Upward acceleration: N = m(g + a)
- Downward acceleration: N = m(g - a)
- Free fall: N = 0 (apparent weightless)

## Friction

### Types of Friction

- **Static friction**: Prevents relative motion
  - f_s ≤ μ_s N
  - Maximum static friction: f_s,max = μ_s N
- **Kinetic friction**: Opposes relative motion
  - f_k = μ_k N
  - μ_k < μ_s (typically)

### Angle of Repose

- Maximum angle before sliding: tan θ = μ_s

### Applications

- Braking distance
- Friction in machines
- Tire grip

## Circular Motion Dynamics

### Centripetal Force

- F_c = mv²/r = mω²r
- Provided by:
  - Tension (pendulum, string)
  - Gravity (satellites)
  - Friction (car turning)
  - Normal force (banked curves)

### Banked Curves

- θ = arctan(v²/(rg))
- No friction needed at ideal speed

## Impulse and Momentum

### Impulse-Momentum Theorem

- J = FΔt = Δp
- Impulse = Force × Time
- Momentum: p = mv

### Conservation of Momentum

- For isolated systems: Σp_initial = Σp_final
- Collisions (elastic and inelastic)
- Center of mass motion

## Problem-Solving Strategy

1. **Draw a free-body diagram**
2. **Identify all forces**
3. **Choose coordinate system**
4. **Apply F = ma in each direction**
5. **Solve for unknown quantities**
6. **Check units and reasonableness**
