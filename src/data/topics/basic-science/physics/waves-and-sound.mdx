# Waves & Sound

## Introduction to Waves

Waves are disturbances that propagate through space and time, transferring
energy without transferring matter. They are fundamental to understanding sound,
light, and many other physical phenomena.



#### Mechanical Waves

- Require a medium for propagation
- Examples: Sound waves, water waves, seismic waves
- Cannot travel through vacuum

#### Electromagnetic Waves

- Do not require a medium
- Can travel through vacuum
- Examples: Light, radio waves, X-rays

#### Matter Waves

- Associated with particles (quantum mechanical)
- Wave-particle duality

#### Transverse Waves

- Particle motion perpendicular to wave propagation
- Examples: Water ripples, electromagnetic waves
- Wave features: Crests (peaks) and troughs (valleys)

#### Longitudinal Waves

- Particle motion parallel to wave propagation
- Examples: Sound waves, seismic P-waves
- Wave features: Compressions and rarefactions

#### Surface Waves

- Combination of transverse and longitudinal
- Particles move in elliptical paths
- Examples: Ocean waves



#### Wavelength (λ)

Distance between two consecutive identical points on a wave (e.g., crest to
crest).

#### Frequency (f)

Number of complete oscillations per second. Unit: Hertz (Hz).

#### Time Period (T)

Time for one complete oscillation.

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
T = 1/f
```

#### Wave Speed (v)

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
v = fλ = λ/T
```

#### Amplitude (A)

Maximum displacement from equilibrium position.

#### Phase

Position of a point in the wave cycle.

### Wave Equation

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
y(x,t) = A sin(kx - ωt)
```

Where:

- k = 2π/λ (wave number)
- ω = 2πf (angular frequency)
- (kx - ωt) = phase

### Angular Quantities

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
ω = 2πf = 2π/T
k = 2π/λ
v = ω/k
```

### Superposition Principle

When two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum
of individual displacements.

#### Constructive Interference

- Waves in phase
- Amplitude adds: A_total = A₁ + A₂
- Path difference = nλ

#### Destructive Interference

- Waves out of phase
- Amplitude subtracts: A_total = |A₁ - A₂|
- Path difference = (n + ½)λ

### Standing Waves

- Formed by superposition of two identical waves traveling in opposite
  directions
- Nodes: Points of zero displacement
- Antinodes: Points of maximum displacement

### Beats

- Interference of two waves with slightly different frequencies
- Beat frequency = |f₁ - f₂|
- Amplitude modulates at beat frequency

### Doppler Effect

Change in observed frequency due to relative motion between source and observer.

#### Doppler Effect Formulas

**Moving Observer:**

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
f' = f(v ± v_o)/v
```

**Moving Source:**

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
f' = f v/(v ∓ v_s)
```

**Both Moving:**

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
f' = f (v ± v_o)/(v ∓ v_s)
```

- f' = observed frequency
- f = source frequency
- v = wave speed in medium
- v_o = observer velocity
- v_s = source velocity

### Applications of Doppler Effect

- Radar and speed guns
- Medical ultrasound
- Astronomy (redshift/blueshift)
- Weather radar

### Energy Transport

- Waves transfer energy without transferring matter
- Energy is proportional to amplitude squared

### Intensity

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
I = P/A
```

- For isotropic waves: I ∝ 1/r²

### Decibel Scale (Sound)

### Characteristics of Sound

- Longitudinal mechanical waves
- Frequency range: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (human hearing)
- Speed in air: ≈ 343 m/s (at 20°C)

#### In Gases

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
v = √(γRT/M)
```

Where γ = C_p/C_v, R = universal gas constant, T = absolute temperature, M =
molar mass

#### In Liquids

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
v = √(B/ρ)
```

Where B = bulk modulus, ρ = density

#### In Solids

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
v = √(Y/ρ)
```

Where Y = Young's modulus, ρ = density

### Factors Affecting Speed of Sound

- **Temperature**: Increases with temperature
- **Density**: Generally inversely proportional
- **Pressure**: No effect for ideal gases (at constant temperature)
- **Humidity**: Increases speed slightly

### Reflection of Sound

- Echo: Reflected sound reaching the ear
- Minimum distance for distinct echo: 17 m
- Reverberation: Multiple reflections

### Refraction of Sound

- Bending of sound waves due to speed variations
- Temperature gradients cause sound to bend
- Explanation for "sound shadows"

### Diffraction of Sound

- Sound waves bend around obstacles
- Wavelength similar to obstacle size
- Easier to hear around corners

### Resonance

- When driving frequency matches natural frequency
- Amplitude becomes maximum
- Applications: Musical instruments, buildings

### Interference of Sound

- Constructive and destructive interference
- Beats from slightly different frequencies
- Sound cancellation (noise-canceling headphones)



#### Pitch

- Determined by frequency
- Higher frequency = higher pitch
- Perception is logarithmic

#### Loudness

- Determined by amplitude/intensity
- Perceived logarithmically (decibels)

#### Timbre (Quality)

- Determined by harmonics/overtones
- Distinguishes instruments playing same note
- Based on wave shape

#### Standing Waves in Strings

Where n = 1,2,3,... (harmonics)

### Musical Instruments

- **String instruments**: Violin, guitar, piano
- **Wind instruments**: Flute, trumpet, clarinet
- **Percussion instruments**: Drums, xylophone

### Definition

Sound waves with frequency > 20,000 Hz (above human hearing)

### Applications

- **Medical imaging**: Diagnostic ultrasound
- **Therapeutic**: Physiotherapy, lithotripsy
- **Industrial**: Non-destructive testing
- **Cleaning**: Ultrasonic cleaners
- **Sonar**: Underwater navigation

### Principles

- Piezoelectric effect for generation/detection
- Pulse-echo method for imaging
- Attenuation limits depth

### Mach Number

// Example demonstrating Waves & Sound.
```
M = v_object/v_sound
```

- M < 1: Subsonic
- M = 1: Sonic
- M > 1: Supersonic

### Shock Waves

- Formed when object exceeds speed of sound
- Compression wave front
- Sonic boom at M > 1

### Huygens' Principle

Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets.

### Diffraction

- Bending of waves around obstacles
- Wavelength determines amount of diffraction
- Diffraction gratings

### Polarization

- Restriction of wave oscillation to one plane
- Only transverse waves can be polarized
- Applications: Sunglasses, photography, LCD screens

### Electromagnetic Spectrum

| ----------- | ---------------- | -------------------------- |
| Radio waves | > 0.1 m          | < 3 × 10⁹ Hz               |
| Microwaves  | 1 mm - 0.1 m     | 3 × 10⁹ - 3 × 10¹¹ Hz      |

| Visible     | 400 - 700 nm     | 4.3 × 10¹⁴ - 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz |
| Ultraviolet | 10 - 400 nm      | 7.5 × 10¹⁴ - 3 × 10¹⁶ Hz   |
| X-rays      | 0.01 - 10 nm     | 3 × 10¹⁶ - 3 × 10¹⁹ Hz     |

### Properties of EM Waves

- Transverse waves
- Travel at speed of light (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)
- Self-propagating (electric and magnetic fields)
- Can travel through vacuum
