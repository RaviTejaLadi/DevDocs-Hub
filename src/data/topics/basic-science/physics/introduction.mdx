# Introduction to Physics

## What is Physics?

Physics is the fundamental branch of science that studies matter, energy, and their interactions. It seeks to understand the basic laws governing the universe, from the smallest subatomic particles to the largest galaxies.

## Main Branches of Physics

### Classical Physics
- **Mechanics**: Study of motion, forces, and energy
- **Thermodynamics**: Study of heat, work, and temperature
- **Optics**: Study of light and its properties
- **Acoustics**: Study of sound and waves
- **Electromagnetism**: Study of electric and magnetic fields

### Modern Physics
- **Quantum Mechanics**: Behavior of matter at atomic and subatomic levels
- **Relativity**: Study of space, time, and gravity
- **Nuclear Physics**: Structure and behavior of atomic nuclei
- **Particle Physics**: Fundamental particles and their interactions
- **Astrophysics**: Physical properties of celestial objects

## Scientific Method in Physics

1. **Observation**: Noticing natural phenomena
2. **Hypothesis**: Formulating testable explanations
3. **Experimentation**: Testing hypotheses through controlled experiments
4. **Analysis**: Interpreting experimental data
5. **Conclusion**: Drawing conclusions and refining theories
6. **Peer Review**: Validation by the scientific community

## Important Physical Constants

- Speed of light: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
- Gravitational constant: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N⋅m²/kg²
- Planck's constant: h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J⋅s
- Avogadro's number: Nₐ = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹
- Boltzmann constant: k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K
